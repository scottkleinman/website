IPA Exercises

This exercise generates random words for you to transcribe into the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA). The answers are designed to familiarise you with with the simplified phonological transcription system we will be using in English 400. They are not phonetic transcriptions and may therefore differ from your actual spoken pronunciation. So do not worry if your transcriptions do not exactly match the suggested answers at first. You will eventually learn the system.

Click Show Answer to check your transcription. Click New Word to generate a new prompt. You may enter your transcription in the form field before checking it against the suggested answer. Clicking the Common IPA Symbols buttons will insert the symbols in the form field. Alternatively, you can write out your transcription before viewing the answer.

  • Vowel length is not indicated since it is sensitive to phonetic context in most Modern English dialects.
  • In words of more than one syllable, the stressed syllable is preceded by ˈ.
  • Historical o which has been unrounded in Modern American dialects (as in God) is generally represented /ɔ/. Words with syllabic consonants in words like button are represented as two syllables with a schwa in the last syllable. In stressed syllables, and occasionally elsewhere, the lowered version of schwa is represented /ʌ/.
  • In a few cases, the answers provide some alternative answers to allow for some common American pronunciations, but this is not done consistently.
